## 1. Current purpose

## 4. Evidence model

### 4a. Labels for every statement

Use these labels on portfolio content, at least while drafting:

| Label | Means | Misuse to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| EVIDENCE | An artifact or record that exists and could be shown: file, screen, prototype, code, dated doc, version history | Describing something that can no longer be shown as if it can |
| CLAIM | A statement about ability, role or result that isn't directly shown | "I led the design" without saying what you did |
| OBSERVATION | Something you saw or heard directly, with source and context (who, how many, when) | Presenting one conversation as "users said" |
| INTERPRETATION | Your reading of observations | Presenting your reading as research findings |
| LEARNING | What changed in your thinking or practice | — |
| OUTCOME | Something that happened in the world because of the work: shipped, used, adopted, decided on by others, measured | Counting "delivered the designs" as an outcome |
| UNKNOWN | Not known, and said out loud | Staying silent so readers fill the gap with an assumption |

*Proposed addition:* **attribution** applies across all the labels. For each statement, was it me, we (and what my part was), or others? Co-founder and team projects need this most.

### 4b. Strength of evidence (proposed, rough)

- **Strong:** artifact from the time, an observable outcome, and a clear role
- **Good:** artifacts from the time (versions, rejected options, decision notes, commits, messages); working things a visitor can use; someone else's corroboration in their own words
- **Weak alone:** explanation written afterwards. You need it to make sense of the rest, but it can't carry the claim alone.

So a rejected direction explained today, with nothing from the time to back it up, is weak evidence on its own.

### 4c. What could count as evidence for each capability

| Capability | Could count | Doesn't count by itself |
|---|---|---|
| Judgment | Options considered, criteria, what was rejected and why, what happened next; a call you got wrong and how you noticed | A hindsight list of good decisions |
| Visual craft | The work at full fidelity; rounds of refinement; consistency across many screens; the site itself | Saying "attention to detail" |
| Simplify / communicate | Complex input next to simple output; the case-study writing; diagrams | A minimal look (minimal isn't the same as simple) |
| Systems thinking | Rules, components, states and tokens used across several surfaces; or maps of actors and flows that changed a decision | A component screenshot with no rules or usage |
| Production-ready | States, edge cases, errors, empty and loading states, responsive layouts, accessibility; handoff; what shipped vs. what was designed | Happy-path mockups |
| Implementation understanding | Constraints found with engineers and how the design changed; trade-offs made for technical reasons | A list of tools; a degree |
| Building with code | Working things people can inspect; source code; commit history | "Familiar with React" |
| Iteration | Versions with what changed and why; what a test showed; what was dropped | One final version under a "process" header |
| Experimentation | A body of small, dated experiments with what each taught, failures included | One polished "experiment" |
| Explaining decisions | Annotated work; written rationale | — |

Tensions to resolve before the information architecture (not now):
- **Curated vs. archive.** A "living body of work" and a hiring manager with little time pull in opposite directions.
- **Showing evolution** means showing older, weaker work. Who is that for?
- **Upkeep.** A living site has to be kept up. A stale "recent" section counts as evidence against you.

## 7. Hypotheses

Format: the belief, and what would weaken it.

- **H1** The thread through your work is judgment, specifically deciding what's worth building and claiming under uncertainty.
  *Weakened if:* the evidence inventory finds it in only one project, your role in the Attrangi decisions was minor, or nothing from the time exists.
- **H2** Product-design hiring managers respond to visible decision-making, not only polished screens.
  *Weakened if:* at your level, visual craft turns out to be the first filter. If it doesn't land in seconds, nobody reads the reasoning.
- **H3** A living, multi-format body of work raises interest rather than diluting it.
  *Weakened if:* more items lower the average quality and weaken the overall impression.
- **H4** A site you code yourself counts as evidence that you can build.
  *Weakened if:* readers treat a personal portfolio site as a baseline first project rather than as evidence.
- **H5** One site can serve hiring now and admissions later without compromising either.
  *Weakened if:* formats, reading behaviour, and what counts as strong differ too much.
- **H6** Breadth (product × founder × creative technology) reads as range when a common thread is visible.
  *Weakened if:* the thread isn't visible in the work itself. Then breadth reads as unfocused.
- **H7** Labelling prototypes, unknowns and gaps honestly raises credibility rather than lowering perceived ability.
  *Weakened if:* readers take candour as weakness. Untested.
- **H8** Attrangi is the strongest anchor project.
  *Unverified.* It depends on role clarity, shareable material and the startup's status (Q5).
- **H9** Small experiments made regularly can partly make up for having no shipped outcomes.
  *Weakened if:* experiments without depth read as dabbling.

## 9. Design principles for the portfolio

Only principles you have stated. **Firm** means you stated it as a rule. **Provisional** means you stated it as current thinking, or there's an unresolved tension.

- **P1 Evidence over assertion** *(firm)*. The site doesn't say what you are (creative, different, passionate). The work has to make that inferable.
- **P2 Credibility rule** *(firm)*. No invented metrics. Prototypes are labelled as prototypes. Interpretation isn't presented as research. Your role is stated precisely, and gaps are named. It's the same test you applied at Attrangi ("does this belong in what we can responsibly claim today?"), now applied to yourself.
- **P3 Not every project becomes a case study** *(firm)*. *Provisional criterion:* the space a project gets follows the strength of its evidence. That criterion is my reading, not yet agreed.
- **P4 No title lock-in** *(firm on intent, provisional in practice)*. Tension: hiring managers hire for a role and may need to know quickly what you're applying as. Not leading with a title shouldn't mean leaving them to guess.
- **P5 The site creates evidence, not only displays it** *(firm on intent)*.
- **P6 Each piece is treated as it needs; no single house aesthetic** *(provisional)*. Tension: range vs. no point of view. "I adapt my craft to the problem" is still a claim and needs evidence like any other.
- **P7 Built to change** *(firm)*. The site is meant to evolve, and so is this document.

## 10. Next discovery questions (before information architecture)

1. **What exists?** Run an evidence inventory, one row per project. Columns: your role, artifacts, which artifacts are from the time, what's shareable, outcomes, and which signals it could support. *Tests H1, H8; covers G2, G6–G8, Q5–Q8, Q11.*
2. **For whom, exactly?** Pick the specific role(s) (Q2). Then find out what those hiring managers check, in what order, and how quickly. *Method:* 3–5 conversations with practising designers or design managers, ideally watching them review real portfolios. *Tests H2, H3.*
3. **What's actually wrong now?** Go through the current site page by page. Label each problem as work, framing or presentation (Q1).
4. **What must land first?** Choose the 2–3 signals (Q4). This is probably answerable only after 1 and 2.
5. **What's coming?** Decide which gaps new projects will close in the next 3–6 months (G1, G3, G5, G11). The information architecture has to make room for work that's coming, not only work that exists.
6. **Constraints.** Time budget, whether to put up an interim shareable version, and admissions formats (Q12–Q14).
